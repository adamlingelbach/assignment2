This project looks into complaints against NYPD officers. The "black hat" visualization is a manipulation to convey the data in a way that is not accurate.

White-hat design write-up

For the white hat visualization, we wanted to clearly show how allegation outcomes differ across four categories of within the NYPD. We used the "Civilian Complaints Against New York City Police Officers" data set. This dataset was compiled by ProPublica and contains civilian complaints filed against NYPD officers. It includes information about the types of alleged misconduct and the outcomes of investigations. For our visualization, we focused specifically on comparing the outcomes of allegations across four misconduct categories. We created a horizontal stacked bar chart that compares Abuse of Authority, Force, Discourtesy, and Offensive Language. The maid difference within this data set is that each category is divided into three possible outcomes: substantiated, unsubstantiated, and exonerated. This was the main design choice that differed with the black-hat because exaunerated really shows a difference, rather than being grouped in with "Unsubstantiated". These outcomes have different meanings, and separating them allows viewers to better understand the results of the allegations.

We chose a horizontal stacked bar chart because it allows viewers to easily compare the proportions of different outcomes across misconduct categories. Rather than displaying the raw number of allegations, we converted the outcomes into percentages so that every bar totals 100%. This makes comparisons easier because some categories may have significantly more allegations than others. We also chose colors that have a high contrast with eachother whic really highlights the difference. The horizontal axis runs from 0% to 100%, ensuring that each category is represented on the same scale. Additionally, we included a dropdown menu that allows users to focus on individual misconduct categories or view all four at once.

Overall, our goal was to make the visualization as clear and accurate as possible. By keeping the three outcomes separate, using consistent percentages, and including clear labels, we allow viewers to interpret the data without being influenced by misleading design choices. We wanted to make sure the visualization accurately represented the differences between misconduct categories while remaining easy for viewers to understand.

BLACK HAT: DELIBERATELY MISLEADING

Most reviewed allegations did not result in substantiation

The misleading techniques we used were 1. data transformation, 2. altering the marks, and 3. adding deceptive titles. See below for more details.

Black-hat manipulation disclosure

ADAM ADD INTRO PARAGRAPH
1. Data Transformation: We decided to aggregate the unsubstantiated and exonerated because the officers were not found guilty of those complaints. There is a subtle difference in the defintions of these terms. Unsubstantiated means there wasn't enough evidence to decide a verdict. Exonerated means that conduct occurred, but it was within acceptable policy. We decided to merge these two categories to be purposefully misleading making it look like the complaints had no evidence behind them, when in fact Exonerated complaints often involved conduct that did occur.
2. Altering the Marks: We changed how the two remaining segments are drawn so the readers eye lands on the outcome we want emphasized. “Not substantiated” is a dark, saturated teal, while “Substantiated” is a pale gray-blue that blends into the white background and reads as leftover space rather than a real outcome. We also placed a bold white percentage label only on the “Not substantiated” segment, so the reader gets an exact number for the dominant bar but has to hover to learn the substantiated rate. Together, these choices make the substantiated share look small and unimportant.
3. Adding Deceptive Titles: The title, “Most reviewed allegations did not result in substantiation,” immediately gives the reader a conclusion before they have a chance to interpret the chart themselves. By using this wording, we guide the reader toward thinking that most complaints against officers were not valid. Combined with the merged categories and altered colors, the title reinforces the message that most complaints did not have enough evidence behind them, even though that is not necessarily what the original data shows.
